# MeterFlow — Time-Series Forecasting Evaluation Report: Linear Run-Rate vs. ARIMA

## 1. Executive Summary & Objective

Based on architectural mentor guidance, this evaluation experiment assesses the forecasting capabilities of MeterFlow's budget and spend tracking subsystem:

> **Mentor Guidance:** "Compare the existing linear run-rate forecasting approach with ARIMA using realistic chronological usage/spending data and report MAPE."

This study provides an evidence-based comparison between:
- **Model A (Production Baseline):** The existing explainable **Linear Run-Rate Extrapolation** model (`backend/src/services/spendForecaster.js`).
- **Model B (Statistical Benchmark):** An Auto-Regressive Integrated Moving Average (**ARIMA**) model (`backend/src/services/forecasting/arimaEvaluator.js`).

Both models were trained and evaluated on an identical, deterministic chronological time series of daily cloud service spending.

---

## 2. Models Under Evaluation

### Model A: Linear Run-Rate Extrapolation (Current Production)
- **Source Module:** `backend/src/services/spendForecaster.js`
- **Mechanism:** Computes historical daily average spend across elapsed active budget days:
  $$\bar{y}_{\text{train}} = \frac{1}{T_{\text{train}}} \sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{train}}} y_t$$
  Extrapolates remaining period spend as:
  $$\hat{Y}_{\text{future}} = \bar{y}_{\text{train}} \times H$$
  where $H$ is the forecast horizon (days remaining).
- **Core Strengths:**
  - Zero external dependencies.
  - $O(1)$ constant-time calculation.
  - Fully transparent and explainable to non-technical finance and operational users.
  - Operates reliably with as few as 2 days of data.
- **Documented Limitations:**
  - Assumes stationary linear burn rate.
  - Does not model weekend vs. weekday demand dips, seasonality, or serial correlation.

### Model B: Auto-Regressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA)
- **Source Module:** `backend/src/services/forecasting/arimaEvaluator.js`
- **Package:** `arima@0.2.8` (Zero-dependency WebAssembly/JavaScript implementation).
- **Configuration:** Order $(p=1, d=1, q=1)$, modeling first-order autoregression and moving-average error adjustments over first-differenced series.
- **Mechanism:** Models stochastic autocorrelations and non-linear momentum over time:
  $$\Delta y_t = c + \phi_1 \Delta y_{t-1} + \theta_1 \epsilon_{t-1} + \epsilon_t$$
- **Core Strengths:**
  - Captures autocorrelation, drift, and short-term trends.
  - Adapts to non-stationary demand patterns.
- **Documented Limitations:**
  - Requires minimum sample size (at least 15–30+ observations) before fitting is viable.
  - Higher computational complexity ($O(k \cdot n)$ parameter estimation).
  - Less explainable to end-users during billing dispute resolutions.

## 3. Evaluation Dataset & Methodology

### 3.1 Dataset Specification
To ensure strict reproducibility and privacy protection, the evaluation utilizes a **deterministic synthetic time series** generated by `backend/src/services/forecasting/dataset.js`.

> **Note on Data Provenance:** This dataset is explicitly **synthetic/simulated evaluation data** generated using a fixed PRNG seed (Seed: 42). It is not real customer production data.

- **Total Observations:** 90 consecutive calendar days (`2026-08-01` to `2026-10-29`).
- **Characteristics Modeled:**
  1. **Base Spend & Growth Trend:** Base spend of \$42.00/day with steady linear growth of +\$0.25/day (simulating expanding API and compute utilization).
  2. **Day-of-Week Seasonality:** Weekday traffic (Monday–Friday) indexed at ~1.15x; weekend traffic (Saturday–Sunday) indexed at ~0.70x–0.75x.
  3. **Deterministic Variation:** Bounded uniform fluctuations in $[-\$3.50, +\$3.50]$ via a Mulberry32 PRNG.
  4. **Periodic Spikes:** Bi-weekly scheduled batch processing runs adding +\$18.50 on every 14th day.

### 3.2 Train / Held-Out Test Split
- **Training Period:** 60 days (`2026-08-01` to `2026-09-29`).
- **Held-Out Test Period:** 30 days (`2026-09-30` to `2026-10-29`).
- **Forecast Horizon ($H$):** 30 days.
- **Evaluation Rule:** Both models train strictly on the 60-day historical window. Neither model has access to observations in the 30-day evaluation window prior to prediction.

---

## 4. Evaluation Metric: Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE)

### 4.1 Definition
$$\text{MAPE} = \frac{100}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \left| \frac{\text{Actual}_i - \text{Forecast}_i}{\text{Actual}_i} \right|$$

### 4.2 Zero-Actual Handling Policy
In real-world billing, a customer may consume \$0 on certain days. To prevent division-by-zero errors without distorting metrics:
- If $\text{Actual}_i = 0$ and $\text{Forecast}_i = 0$, relative error is $0.0\%$.
- If $\text{Actual}_i = 0$ and $\text{Forecast}_i \neq 0$, the denominator is clamped to $\epsilon = 0.01$ (one cent, the minimum monetary currency unit).
- For this 90-day simulation, all daily spend observations are positive ($\ge \$0.50$).

## 6. Analysis & Architectural Trade-offs

1. **Why ARIMA Performed Better:**
   - **Trend Adaptation:** Linear run-rate calculated the historical 60-day average (\$51.48/day) and projected it horizontally. Because spending grew by +\$0.25/day, actual test-period spend averaged \$64.44/day. The linear run-rate underestimated the cumulative spend (\$1,544.40 vs. \$1,933.20).
   - **Autoregressive Memory:** ARIMA(1,1,1) differenced the series ($d=1$) to remove the upward trend and leveraged the autoregressive term ($\phi_1$) to project closer to recent end-of-quarter values (~$62.20/day), capturing \$1,865.93 of the \$1,933.20 total.
   - **Error Reduction:** ARIMA reduced MAPE from **24.63% to 17.27%**, an absolute improvement of **7.36%** (29.88% relative error reduction).

2. **Why Linear Run-Rate Remains Essential in Production:**
   - **Immediate Usability:** Linear run-rate works from Day 2 of any budget cycle. ARIMA requires at least 15–30 continuous daily observations, making it unavailable for new workspaces or the first week of any billing month.
   - **Zero Compute Overhead:** The linear calculation executes in sub-millisecond time inside standard MongoDB aggregation pipelines or Express request lifecycles.
   - **Explainability:** Customers demand predictable explanations for why a budget threshold alert was triggered (e.g. `Actual Spend ($850) / Elapsed Days (10) * Total Days (30) = $2,550`). Complex ARIMA polynomial weights cannot be easily conveyed on an invoice or alert email.

---

## 7. Recommendation & Conclusion

- **Production Decision:** **Retain Model A (Linear Run-Rate) as the core operational forecasting engine** in `backend/src/services/spendForecaster.js` to preserve real-time budget evaluation and zero-dependency reliability.
- **Evaluation Benchmark:** Provide Model B (ARIMA) via the evaluation suite (`npm run evaluate:forecast`) for offline planning and future opt-in enterprise analytics pipelines.
- **Next Step:** As workspaces accumulate $> 60$ days of history, offer ARIMA/SARIMA as an advanced secondary forecast overlay in the reporting dashboard.
